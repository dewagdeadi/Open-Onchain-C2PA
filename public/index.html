How it works

Many cameras and editing apps now attach Content Credentials to photos: a signed note saying who made the file and how it was edited. This site adds a permanent public record of those notes, so anyone can tell whether they're still the same as when they were recorded.

1

A publisher records

When a photo is published, its Content Credentials are recorded in a public archive that can't be edited or deleted.

2

You check

Drop the photo here. Your device reads its credentials and sends only their fingerprints to look them up.

3

You see the history

When it was recorded, whether anything changed since, and whether the publisher later withdrew it.

Questions

Do I need cryptocurrency or a wallet?

No. Checking is free and needs no account. Publishers don't need crypto either: the site operator handles recording.

Is my photo uploaded when I check it?

No. Your browser reads the file on your device and sends only fingerprints (short codes computed from it). The photo itself never leaves your device.

What is the "public archive"?

It's Arweave, a network that stores data permanently on many independent computers. Once something is recorded there, nobody (including us) can change or remove it, which is what makes the record trustworthy.

Does "recorded" mean the photo is real?

No. It proves who signed the credentials and that they haven't changed since a certain date. Whether the scene is genuine is still a matter of trust in the publisher.

What if a photo isn't found?

That doesn't make it fake. Most photos are never recorded here. Its Content Credentials can still be checked with other tools, such as contentcredentials.org/verify.

Why did my photo show "No Content Credentials"?

Social networks and messaging apps often strip them when you share a file. Try the original file, or use Search with a watermark code if the publisher added one.
